correction route, controlled fault injection, and risk-based review tiers.

Acceptance:
1. A qualifying formatting/newline correction retains prior substantive reviews,
   gets a new immutable identity and one independent correction verification;
   semantic/authority/unknown changes cannot use that route.
2. A deterministic trigger can be controlled at a stated boundary, without
   substituting the production contract or using its simulation as the oracle.
   Relevant real-resource and deliberately bad-behavior controls remain required.
3. Tier selection follows maximum direct/indirect contract risk, including config,
   generated data, tests and governance; location and size do not lower the tier.
4. No waived failing criterion, implicit source opening, evidence rewriting,
   consumed-owner reuse, new invocation authority, lost floor test, or automatic
   transfer of approval to new bytes. Prior specific task contracts still bind.

Mechanisms and risks: narrowly qualify mechanical corrections against a pinned
substantive review anchor; inspect the full cumulative delta to prevent chained
scope laundering. Separate trigger injection from production effects and an
independent oracle to prevent self-confirmation. Classify risk before implementation
and let reviewers challenge it to prevent convenient low-tier declarations.
Alternatives rejected: accepting unresolved style errors, disabling evidence
safeguards, requiring OS allocation luck, or classifying all documentation as safe.

Dependency: only adoption of these prospective rules waits on this task. Other
lanes remain governed by their existing dispositions; none is opened here.
Budget: four repository files; no new proof framework; one initial text round and
at most one correction before controller direction. Aim for under 300 manually
added/removed lines excluding generated STATUS. A material new policy question,
scope expansion or repeated residual stops the round rather than inventing a rule.
